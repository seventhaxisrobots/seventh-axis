01 · The problem

Malaysian factories must automate faster than they can program robots.

Labour is getting dearer by policy. The minimum wage rose to RM1,700 a month in 2025, manufacturers pay RM1,850 a year in levy for each foreign worker, and a tiered levy is coming. Malaysia has about 74 industrial robots per 10,000 manufacturing workers, against 98 in Thailand and 818 in Singapore.

The arm itself is no longer the obstacle. A cobot cell costs USD 40,000 to 150,000, and the Smart Automation Grant has matched up to 70% of eligible spending. What stops a mid-sized plant is programming: every new task needs a specialist, high-mix lines change every week, and managers fear the first run of a new program.

So robots get bought for one job and left on it, and plants stay manual because changing a robot costs more than hiring.

02 · The product

The AI never moves the robot.

A language model turns the operator's words into a plan. Our own code checks it, a named person approves it, and only that signed program runs. The server and the bridge enforce the order, not just the screen.

  1. AskType or say the task in BM, English or Mandarin.
  2. CheckReach, collisions, payload and speed near people, against the real cell.
  3. ApproveWatch it run in 3D, then a named person signs that exact program.
  4. RunThe bridge beside the cell runs it, and refuses anything unsigned.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what we do about it.

Pilots don't show a clear payback
We choose sites with frequent changeovers, agree what to measure before we start and publish the payback report. If fewer than half of the first 10 pilots convert, we stop and rethink.
A robot maker builds the same thing into its pendant
We work across cells and brands, keep the approval record, speak BM and Mandarin well, and aim to be the integrators' tool before it happens.
Someone is hurt at a cell running our program
Checks and simulation before every run, signed approval by a named person, and the controller's own safety system always in charge. Product liability insurance from the first pilot.
Integrators see us as a threat
They earn 20% of every subscription they bring, for life, and keep their installation business. We never install or sell hardware.
Why READY Robotics failed, and why we won't
It raised over USD 41M for a universal robot OS that needed extra hardware and a new language. We need neither, and we work with robot makers' channels, not against them.
One robot brand today
Universal Robots leads SME cobot cells, so we start there. A second brand follows in 2028; the bridge is built so new brands plug in.
