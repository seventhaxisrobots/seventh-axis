From words to motion

  1. Plan

    You describe the task. The planner turns it into named steps you can read, not a black box.

  2. Simulate

    The plan runs on a digital twin of your cell with the real arm's kinematics and joint limits.

  3. Approve

    A person on your team reviews the motion and the checks, then approves it. Or doesn't.

  4. Export

    Only approved plans become robot code, which runs inside the safety system your integrator set up.

What we check, every time

Reach and joint limits
Every target is solved with the arm's real inverse kinematics. Out of reach means the plan stops there.
Collisions
Paths are checked against the cell's fixtures, pallets, conveyor and the objects already placed.
Payload
Object weights are compared with the arm and gripper rating before anything is picked.
People nearby
In simulation, the arm slows when someone enters the warning zone and stops in the protective zone.
Stack stability
Layers, heights and placements are checked so a pallet pattern is buildable, not just drawable.
Honest failure
When a request isn't possible, the planner says why instead of guessing. Unclear instructions get a question back.

It looks before it picks.

The infeed camera labels every box by colour and flags damage, so a crushed or leaking box goes to the reject bin instead of onto a customer's pallet.

Standards and responsibility

Seventh Axis is software. It doesn't replace your cell's safety system, and it doesn't certify a cell. Guarding, light curtains, area scanners and the risk assessment stay with your integrator, following ISO 10218 for industrial robots and ISO/TS 15066 where people work alongside collaborative arms.

Exported programs run inside the limits the robot controller already enforces. Our checks are an extra layer on top, so problems are caught at a desk instead of on the floor.

The Studio playground can throw and toss boxes for fun. Those moves are simulation-only and can never be exported.
